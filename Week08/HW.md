## Use Tuesday's lesson

Everything from Tuesday applies directly to the midterm, and all three parts
are quick the second time:

- [ ] **Host it.** Get the midterm onto **GitHub Pages** and put the live link
      in your `Midterm/README.md` and in your repo's main `README.md`. You did
      this in class; the second time takes five minutes.
- [ ] **Pull your colours into CSS variables.** Find the hex codes you have
      repeated, name them at `:root`, and swap them for `var()`. Cheapest
      possible improvement to a stylesheet you are still changing your mind
      about.
- [ ] **Add the contact form** — a contact page, or a section on the home page.
      Your own colours, which now means redefining variables rather than
      rewriting rules.

## Bring it up to standard

- [ ] **Responsive on a real phone**, not just a narrow browser window. Open it
      on your actual phone.
- [ ] **Every page linked and reachable.** No dead nav items, no `href="#"` left
      over from week 2.
- [ ] **Real `alt` text on every image.**
- [ ] **Images sized sensibly.** A 4000px photo scaled down in CSS is still a
      4000px download — the browser fetches the whole thing and then shrinks it.
      Resize each image to roughly the size it actually displays at and export
      it again (Preview, Squoosh, Photoshop, whatever you have), then note the
      before and after folder size in your README. You should see a real
      difference, and on a phone connection your visitors will feel it.
- [ ] **`README.md` in your `Midterm` folder** describing the concept, with your
      wireframe or sitemap and the live link.
